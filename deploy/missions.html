Historic Missions

Where humanity took its first steps into the cosmos

Space exploration has achieved remarkable milestones that have shaped our understanding of the universe:

  • Apollo 11 (1969): First human landing on the Moon - "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."
  • Voyager 1 & 2 (1977): Interstellar probes carrying the Golden Record with sounds and images of Earth
  • Hubble Space Telescope (1990): Revolutionized astronomy with stunning images of distant galaxies and nebulae
  • Mars Rovers: Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance exploring the Red Planet
  • Cassini-Huygens (1997-2017): Explored Saturn and its moons, especially Titan

Current Missions

Pushing the boundaries of discovery, one mission at a time

Today, numerous missions are actively exploring space. The James Webb Space Telescope is revealing the early universe, the Parker Solar Probe is studying the Sun up close, and multiple Mars missions are searching for signs of past life. The International Space Station continues to serve as a laboratory in microgravity, while missions like Juno study Jupiter and New Horizons explores the Kuiper Belt. These missions are expanding our knowledge every day.

Future Missions

Tomorrow's discoveries begin with today's bold ambitions

Exciting missions are planned for the coming decades. NASA's Artemis program aims to return humans to the Moon and establish a sustainable presence. The Europa Clipper will explore Jupiter's moon Europa, which may harbor life in its subsurface ocean. Missions to Mars will continue, with plans for sample return and eventual human exploration. The search for exoplanets and signs of life beyond Earth continues with new telescopes and missions.
