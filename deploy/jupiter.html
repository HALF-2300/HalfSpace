Did You Know?

Jupiter is so massive that all other planets could fit inside it!

The Gas Giant

Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System, a gas giant with a mass greater than all other planets combined. It's composed primarily of hydrogen and helium, similar to the Sun, but it's not massive enough to become a star. Jupiter's rapid rotation creates strong winds and massive storms, including the famous Great Red Spot—a storm larger than Earth that has been raging for at least 400 years. A day on Jupiter is only 10 hours long!

Moons of Jupiter

Jupiter has at least 95 known moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io (volcanically active), Europa (with a subsurface ocean that might harbor life), Ganymede (the largest moon in the Solar System), and Callisto (heavily cratered). These moons are worlds in their own right and are targets for future exploration, especially Europa, which may have conditions suitable for life in its subsurface ocean.

Jupiter's Role

Jupiter acts as a 'cosmic vacuum cleaner,' using its massive gravity to capture or deflect comets and asteroids that might otherwise threaten inner planets like Earth. However, it can also fling objects toward the inner Solar System. Understanding Jupiter helps us learn about gas giants, planetary formation, and the early Solar System. The Juno mission is currently studying Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, and interior structure.
